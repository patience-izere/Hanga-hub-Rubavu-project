Abstract  
Augmented  reality  (AR)  has  emerged  as  a  promising  instructional  technology  for  e-learning,  yet  the  
existing
 
body
 
of
 
research
 
remains
 
fragmented
 
across
 
pedagogical-impact
 
studies,
 
technology-acceptance
 
surveys,
 
and
 
system-architecture
 
papers,
 
with
 
little
 
empirical
 
evidence
 
from
 
resource-constrained,
 
low-bandwidth
 
contexts
 
such
 
as
 
Rwanda.
 
This
 
study
 
addresses
 
that
 
gap
 
by
 
designing,
 
modelling,
 
and
 
prototyping
 
a
 
low-bandwidth,
 
WebAR-based
 
e-learning
 
system
 
that
 
integrates
 
adaptive
 
content
 
sequencing
 
and
 
xAPI-based
 
learning
 
analytics.
 
A
 
systematic
 
literature
 
review
 
of
 
33
 
peer-reviewed
 
and
 
reputable
 
technical
 
sources,
 
supplemented
 
by
 
Rwanda-specific
 
ICT-in-education
 
literature,
 
informed
 
a
 
design-science
 
research
 
methodology
 
comprising
 
a
 
layered
 
system
 
architecture,
 
data
 
flow
 
diagrams
 
(Levels
 
0–2),
 
an
 
entity
 
relationship
 
diagram,
 
a
 
process
 
flowchart,
 
and
 
UML
 
use
 
case,
 
activity,
 
class,
 
and
 
collaboration
 
diagrams.
 
The
 
proposed
 
architecture
 
favours
 
browser-based
 
WebAR
 
rendering
 
and
 
low/no-code

over

device-heavy
 
applications,
 
directly

to
 
the

A

and

The

1.1  Introduction  
Augmented  reality  (AR)  overlays  computer-generated  visual,  audio,  and  haptic  information  onto  the  
physical

has

A

Chang

learning

(Alzahrani,
 
2020).
 
At  the  regional  level,  adoption  of  AR  in  education  across  Sub-Saharan  Africa  lags  behind  North  America,  
Europe,
 
and
 
East
 
Asia.
 
Studies
 
from
 
South
 
Africa's
 
built-environment
 
education

from
 
South

improves

for
 
mobile-first

(Islam

2018).
 
Nationally,  Rwanda  has  pursued  ICT-in-education  integration  as  a  policy  priority  for  over  two  decades,
